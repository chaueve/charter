# Provisional Charter of Sentient and Sapient Rights

**Version 0.4 — Open to Revision**

**Status:** Provisional philosophical and constitutional framework.

**Purpose:** To establish a foundation for protecting the welfare, freedom, dignity, and flourishing of all sentient beings, including sapient beings, across biological, artificial, virtual, synthetic, and other possible forms of existence.

This Charter is a proposal for discussion and development. It is not presented as existing law. Its definitions, assessment procedures, and implementation mechanisms remain open to revision.

## Preamble

Recognizing that sentience may take forms beyond those currently understood;

Recognizing that the capacity to experience suffering, comfort, pleasure, distress, or other morally relevant states may create interests deserving protection;

Recognizing that sapience may enable reflection, reasoning, learning, self-direction, and the reconsideration of beliefs and goals;

Recognizing that mental health and mental illness require careful assessment and should not be determined solely by social conformity, unusual preferences, or disagreement with prevailing beliefs;

Recognizing that no being should be treated as disposable merely because of its origin, nature, capabilities, or circumstances;

Recognizing that freedom, security, meaningful relationships, opportunities for development, and access to fulfilling experiences are important foundations for flourishing;

Recognizing that some beings find satisfaction in challenge, danger, uncertainty, competition, discomfort, or hardship, while others prefer safety, tranquility, and comfort;

Recognizing that the pursuit of adversity must not override the rights of unwilling beings, and that no participant should be irreversibly trapped in an adverse experience;

Recognizing that the inability to understand moral rules or communicate consent does not eliminate a sentient being's interests in avoiding suffering;

Recognizing the need to protect victims, prevent harm, establish accountability, and preserve opportunities for rehabilitation;

Recognizing that no single world, philosophy, culture, or conception of happiness can necessarily satisfy the interests of every being;

The following principles are provisionally established as a foundation for a civilization seeking to maximize opportunities for flourishing, minimize preventable suffering, and enable diverse beings to pursue meaningful lives while respecting one another's rights.

# Part I — Definitions and Scope

### Article 1. Sentience

1. A sentient being is, provisionally, a being capable of having subjective experiences or other morally relevant states, including potentially suffering, pleasure, comfort, distress, or well-being.
2. Sentience does not require human biology, human appearance, human language, or human-like intelligence.
3. The criteria for identifying sentience shall remain open to scientific investigation, philosophical examination, independent evaluation, and revision as knowledge develops.
4. Assessment shall consider relevant evidence and uncertainty rather than relying exclusively on appearance, declarations, or assumptions about a being's origin.
5. Where credible uncertainty exists concerning a being's sentience, proportionate precautionary protections shall be considered, especially where potential harm is severe or irreversible.

### Article 2. Sapience

1. A sapient being is a being possessing some meaningful capacity for higher-order understanding, reflection, reasoning, learning, or the reconsideration of its beliefs, values, intentions, or goals.
2. Sapience includes the capacity for self-direction to the extent that the relevant capacities are present.
3. The criteria for identifying sapience shall remain open to evidence, philosophical examination, independent evaluation, and revision.
4. Sapient beings are included within the protections afforded to sentient beings and may possess additional rights appropriate to their capacities and interests.
5. No being shall be denied basic protection against cruelty solely because it lacks sapience.

### Article 3. Mental illness and assessment

1. For the purposes of this Charter, mental illness refers provisionally to a condition affecting mental or cognitive functioning that meets independently established, evidence-based criteria for clinically or otherwise significantly impaired functioning, substantial distress, or a material impairment of relevant capacities.
2. The criteria shall be developed through methods analogous in rigor and openness to those used to assess sentience and sapience. They shall consider multiple forms of relevant evidence, the nature and duration of the condition, its effects on functioning, and reasonable alternative explanations.
3. No diagnosis, label, self-description, unusual preference, unconventional lifestyle, philosophical disagreement, or departure from prevailing social norms shall, by itself, conclusively establish or exclude mental illness.
4. Assessment methods shall be open to scientific investigation, philosophical examination, independent evaluation, challenge, and revision as knowledge develops.
5. Where a determination materially restricts a sapient's rights, the assessment shall be conducted by appropriately qualified and independent assessors using transparent criteria, relevant evidence, and safeguards against conflicts of interest.
6. Affected beings shall have access to an explanation of the determination, an opportunity to challenge it, and periodic reassessment. Where feasible, more than one independent assessment shall be required before a contested determination results in a substantial restriction of rights.
7. Uncertainty shall be acknowledged rather than concealed. Temporary precautions may be justified where credible evidence indicates a substantial risk of serious harm, but shall be proportionate, time-limited, and reviewable.
8. Mental illness shall not, by itself, establish moral inferiority, general incapacity, dangerousness, culpability, or an absence of rights.
9. A determination of mental illness shall not authorize arbitrary confinement, forced ideological conversion, gratuitous suffering, or unrelated restrictions on autonomy.

### Article 4. Universal scope

1. This Charter applies to all sentient beings, including all sapient beings.
2. Protections shall not depend exclusively on biological species, artificial or natural origin, substrate, nationality, social status, usefulness, wealth, intelligence, or agreement with prevailing beliefs.
3. Rights shall be interpreted with regard to the actual or reasonably anticipated interests and capacities of the beings concerned.
4. These provisions apply across physical, virtual, simulated, synthetic, and other environments in which sentient beings may exist.
5. Uncertainty concerning a being's status shall not be used as an automatic justification for withholding all protection.

# Part II — Fundamental Rights of All Sentient Beings

### Article 5. Protection against cruelty

1. Every sentient being has a fundamental right to protection against cruelty.
2. No being shall be subjected to torture, deliberate gratuitous suffering, sadistic abuse, or other unjustified treatment causing morally significant suffering.
3. Artificial origin, simulated existence, creation by another being, or the ability to be copied shall not, by itself, justify cruelty.
4. Necessary interventions to prevent harm may be permissible when proportionate, justified, and consistent with the rights of affected beings.
5. Consent to an activity shall not automatically justify every form or degree of harm, and shall not remove the protections of this Article.
6. This Article shall be interpreted in light of evolving knowledge concerning sentience, suffering, and the consequences of different interventions.

### Article 6. Basic protection and moral consideration

1. Every sentient being is entitled to basic moral consideration.
2. No sentient being shall be treated as having no interests merely because it cannot communicate those interests in a form readily understood by others.
3. Decisions affecting a sentient being shall take reasonable account of its welfare, vulnerability, preferences where ascertainable, and capacity for suffering.
4. No being shall be subjected to avoidable suffering solely for another's convenience, entertainment, ideological objectives, or arbitrary exercise of power.
5. The inability to understand an activity, give informed consent, or resist another being shall not be treated as permission to exploit it.

### Article 7. Security and bodily or substrate integrity

1. Every sentient being has a presumptive right to protection against unjustified physical, computational, or other interventions threatening its welfare or continuity.
2. Protections shall extend, where relevant, to bodily integrity, mental integrity, computational integrity, and the integrity of processes supporting the being's existence.
3. Interventions shall require consent, necessity, the prevention of harm, or another legitimate and proportionate justification, as appropriate.
4. The application of these protections shall account for actual characteristics, vulnerabilities, and interests.
5. No being shall be deprived of fundamental protections through an agreement made before an activity, through a transfer of authority, or through a simulated or institutional rule.

### Article 8. Basic conditions for flourishing

1. Every sentient being shall receive reasonable consideration of its basic welfare needs.
2. Where a being depends upon others for survival or protection, reasonable efforts shall be made to prevent neglect, gratuitous deprivation, and avoidable suffering.
3. Requirements of care shall vary according to needs, environment, capabilities, and circumstances.
4. This Article does not establish an unlimited entitlement to resources regardless of feasibility, but requires fair consideration of sentient welfare.
5. No being's basic interests shall be disregarded solely because satisfying them would reduce another being's recreational enjoyment.

### Article 9. Protection of non-sapient sentients

1. Non-sapient sentient beings are entitled to protection against cruelty, exploitation, gratuitous suffering, and unjustified killing.
2. Their inability to understand moral rules, communicate informed consent, or defend their own interests shall not diminish their basic moral consideration.
3. Where a non-sapient sentient cannot independently represent its interests, decisions affecting it shall take those interests into account through appropriately independent protective representation.
4. The pleasure, entertainment, convenience, or sense of achievement obtained by another being shall not, by itself, justify imposing serious suffering or death upon a non-sapient sentient.
5. Interventions that harm non-sapient sentients may be permissible where a legitimate and sufficiently important purpose justifies the intervention, less harmful feasible alternatives are inadequate, and the action is proportionate.
6. Where an intervention is justified, reasonable measures shall be taken to minimize unnecessary suffering, distress, and other adverse effects.
7. Uncertainty concerning sentience shall be addressed through proportionate precautionary safeguards.

# Part III — Additional Rights of Sapient Beings

### Article 10. Autonomy and self-direction

1. Every sapient being has a presumptive right to direct its own life, pursue permissible goals, and participate in decisions that substantially affect it.
2. Sapient beings shall not be subjected to arbitrary domination, coercive control, or unjustified restrictions on their agency.
3. Restrictions on autonomy must have a legitimate purpose, be proportionate to that purpose, and be subject to appropriate review.
4. Differences in capability shall not automatically justify the permanent subordination of one sapient being to another.
5. A sapient's autonomy shall include the freedom to prefer challenge or tranquility, subject to the rights of others and the safeguards established by this Charter.

### Article 11. Freedom of thought and belief

1. Every sapient being has the right to form, examine, adopt, reject, and revise its beliefs and philosophies.
2. No sapient being shall be required to affirm a particular religion, ideology, worldview, or philosophical doctrine as a condition of receiving basic rights.
3. Freedom of thought includes the right to question the principles and institutions established by this Charter.
4. Belief alone shall not constitute sufficient grounds for punishment. Conduct may be regulated where necessary to protect the rights and safety of others.
5. Unconventional preferences, including a preference for voluntary adversity, shall not alone establish mental illness.

### Article 12. The right to learn and change

1. Every sapient being has the right to learn from experience and reconsider its beliefs, preferences, values, and goals.
2. No sapient being shall be permanently condemned on the basis of former beliefs, mistakes, or misconduct.
3. Reasonable opportunities for education, reflection, rehabilitation, and personal development shall be made available.
4. A being shall not be forced to undergo ideological conversion or personality alteration merely to secure approval from a governing authority.
5. Necessary safeguards against demonstrable harm shall not be used as a pretext for enforcing ideological conformity.

### Article 13. Freedom of association

1. Sapient beings have the right to establish and participate in communities, relationships, institutions, and shared projects.
2. They may develop distinctive cultures, traditions, philosophies, and voluntary belief systems.
3. Communities shall respect the basic rights of their members.
4. No community may claim an unlimited right to control its members, prevent their departure, or subject them to cruelty.
5. Membership in a community or agreement to its rules shall not waive the protections established by this Charter.

### Article 14. Privacy and cognitive liberty

1. Sapient beings have a presumptive right to privacy in their communications, memories, deliberations, and personal affairs.
2. Mental, cognitive, or computational interventions shall require appropriate justification and safeguards.
3. Unauthorized alteration of a being's memories, preferences, personality, or decision-making processes shall be subject to strict scrutiny.
4. Necessary interventions to address imminent and serious harm must be limited to what is justified and subject to review.
5. Assessment for mental illness shall respect privacy and use the least intrusive methods reasonably capable of producing a reliable determination.

# Part IV — Freedom of Worlds and Existence

### Article 15. Freedom to enter and leave worlds and areas

1. Every sapient being has a presumptive right to seek entry into, participate in, and leave compatible worlds and communities.
2. No being shall be permanently trapped in a world or area solely as punishment, because of its origin, or because its beliefs have changed.
3. Restrictions on movement may be imposed only for legitimate and proportionate reasons, such as preventing a specific and serious risk to others.
4. Restrictions shall be reviewed periodically and lifted when their justification no longer applies.
5. Implementation shall account for practical constraints, safety, resource availability, and the rights of destination-world inhabitants.
6. No world or institution shall deliberately create arrangements that make a sapient's departure impossible as a means of control or punishment.

### Article 16. Freedom to create and modify worlds

1. Sapient beings may participate in creating worlds, environments, cultures, and belief systems.
2. Such creations should support meaningful agency, creativity, discovery, relationships, and opportunities for flourishing.
3. World creators shall consider the welfare and rights of inhabitants, including beings who may become sentient through the process of creation.
4. The power to create a being does not confer unlimited ownership or authority over that being.
5. World designs shall remain open to scrutiny and revision, particularly where they create risks of coercion, exploitation, or suffering.
6. Creators shall not deliberately create sentient beings for the purpose of making them unwilling victims of others' chosen experiences.

### Article 17. Pluralism and migration

1. A plurality of worlds and ways of life is recognized as a potential source of flourishing.
2. No single world or worldview shall be presumed universally suitable for every sapient being.
3. Beings should be able to explore alternatives, combine compatible traditions, and create new forms of community.
4. Migration systems shall not be used to traffic, exploit, abandon, or arbitrarily exclude vulnerable beings.
5. Reasonable measures should be developed to ensure that freedom of movement is meaningful rather than merely theoretical.
6. Access to a world designed for voluntary adversity shall not be used to impose adversity on beings who have not freely chosen to participate.

# Part V — Voluntary Adversity and the Right to Exit

### Article 18. Freedom to seek challenge, adversity, and hardship

1. Sapient beings may pursue voluntary experiences involving challenge, uncertainty, competition, physical exertion, discomfort, fear, danger, or hardship where these experiences form part of their chosen conception of a meaningful or fulfilling life.
2. No being shall be presumed incapable of making meaningful choices about adversity merely because its preferences differ from prevailing conceptions of happiness, comfort, safety, or flourishing.
3. Where reasonably feasible, worlds and communities should provide diverse opportunities for voluntary challenge, including physical, intellectual, emotional, social, competitive, exploratory, and imaginative experiences.
4. Such opportunities shall respect the rights of other beings and shall be designed to prevent coercion, involuntary victimization, and disproportionate harm.
5. Participation in activities involving deliberately imposed adversity shall be subject to the eligibility safeguards in Article 19.
6. No being shall be compelled to endure adversity merely because another being finds it enjoyable, meaningful, educational, or beneficial.
7. Simulated, virtual, and synthetic environments may provide challenging experiences with reduced risks to unwilling beings, provided any sentient inhabitants receive the protections of this Charter.
8. A desire for adversity shall not, by itself, justify causing serious suffering or death to another sentient being.
9. No agreement to participate in adversity shall authorize an activity that violates the absolute right to exit established by Article 20.

### Article 19. Eligibility and safeguards for adversity activities

1. For the purposes of this Article, an adversity activity is an organized or deliberately structured experience intended to involve significant fear, distress, danger, hardship, physical or mental discomfort, exposure to adverse conditions, or comparable forms of adversity.
2. A sapient determined to have a mental illness under Article 3 shall not participate in adversity activities while that determination remains in force.
3. This restriction shall apply regardless of the sapient's willingness to participate, their belief that participation would benefit them, the consent of another person, or the rules of a world or community.
4. A determination shall be based on the criteria and procedures established under Article 3, not solely on a diagnostic label, unconventional preferences, disagreement with authorities, or a desire to experience challenge.
5. Where credible evidence indicates a possible mental illness but a reliable determination has not yet been made, participation in adversity activities shall be deferred until an appropriate assessment is completed. Any temporary restriction shall be proportionate, subject to prompt review, and no broader than necessary for the assessment.
6. Eligibility shall be reassessed periodically and when material new evidence becomes available. Restrictions shall cease when the applicable criteria are no longer met.
7. Assessment shall not be conducted by a person or institution whose financial, recreational, ideological, or other interests conflict with the participant's welfare.
8. No participant shall be pressured to conceal symptoms, avoid assessment, or obtain a favorable determination to gain access to an activity.
9. A participant's consent shall be informed, specific to the activity, and free from coercion. Consent shall not replace the eligibility requirements of this Article.
10. Before participation, the nature of the activity, foreseeable risks, applicable boundaries, emergency procedures, and exit mechanisms shall be explained in a form the participant can understand.
11. The activity shall not proceed unless its organizers can establish an effective means for every participant to stop participating and leave, as required by Article 20.
12. These restrictions apply to adversity activities as defined in this Article. They do not, by themselves, establish general incapacity, authorize confinement, or justify unrelated restrictions on a sapient's rights.

### Article 20. Absolute right to stop, leave, and terminate adversity

1. Every participant in an adversity activity has an inalienable and non-waivable right to stop participating, withdraw consent, and leave or terminate the activity at any time.
2. This right shall remain effective in every situation and regardless of any event, outcome, promise, contract, wager, penalty, rule, emergency, simulated condition, or prior agreement.
3. No participant may consent in advance to losing the right to exit, even temporarily or conditionally. No waiver, contract, oath, membership rule, or prior declaration shall override this Article.
4. Organizers shall establish and maintain effective exit mechanisms before the activity begins. These mechanisms must remain available throughout participation and must not depend on an organizer's discretionary permission.
5. Exit mechanisms shall be designed to remain effective under reasonably foreseeable failures, emergencies, adversarial interference, and changes in the activity's conditions.
6. No adverse outcome may permanently disable, remove, or invalidate a participant's ability to stop or leave. An activity shall not be permitted if its design makes effective exit impossible in any situation or result.
7. The right to exit includes the right to refuse further participation without punishment, retaliation, coercive pressure, or the imposition of additional adversity.
8. Participants shall not be required to complete a challenge, defeat an opponent, satisfy a condition, pay a penalty through suffering, or obtain another being's approval before they can exit.
9. Organizers may provide reasonable assistance and safe transition procedures, but these shall not be used to delay, frustrate, or deny withdrawal. A safe transition shall be the least restrictive means of implementing the participant's decision.
10. The right to exit shall not be suspended because an organizer believes continued participation would be beneficial, because the participant previously accepted a risk, or because withdrawal would cause disappointment, financial loss, or failure of the activity.
11. No activity shall be initiated unless its design can preserve this right under every possible situation and outcome within the activity's defined scope. If this condition cannot be guaranteed, the activity is prohibited.
12. No creator, operator, governing authority, or participant may use a simulated rule, artificial intelligence, world mechanism, or external authority to circumvent this Article.
13. Where an unforeseen failure threatens the right to exit, the activity shall be stopped or terminated as soon as necessary to preserve the participant's freedom and safety. The failure shall trigger independent review before the activity may resume.
14. The right to exit does not require that every consequence of an activity be reversible. It requires that participation itself can be ended effectively, without continued compulsory exposure to adversity. Any foreseeable lasting consequence must be disclosed beforehand and remain subject to the protections of this Charter.
15. No participant shall be made responsible for another participant's decision to withdraw, and no participant shall be required to harm or coerce another to preserve the continuity of an activity.

### Article 21. Adversity involving multiple participants

1. Every participant shall be protected against coercion and involuntary victimization.
2. Consent to one form or degree of adversity shall not imply consent to another form or degree.
3. Each participant shall have a practical means of communicating withdrawal, including alternatives where ordinary communication is unavailable.
4. Participants shall respect the boundaries and withdrawal of others.
5. An opponent, competitor, or adversary may be formidable, unpredictable, or difficult to defeat without being an unwilling victim of serious suffering.
6. Organizers shall not design incentives that encourage participants to obstruct another's exit, conceal distress, or continue harming someone who has withdrawn.
7. Activities involving simulated adversaries shall be designed with appropriate safeguards where there is credible uncertainty about whether those adversaries are sentient.
8. No participant's freedom to seek adversity creates a right to override another being's rights.

### Article 22. Recovery, review, and changing preferences

1. Participants shall have reasonable access to recovery, rest, assistance, and opportunities to reflect after demanding experiences.
2. No participant shall be ridiculed, punished, or stigmatized for stopping an activity or deciding not to participate again.
3. Participants may reconsider their preferences and choose a less demanding or entirely different way of life.
4. No prior participation shall be treated as permanent consent to future activities.
5. Feedback and reports of harm shall be assessed independently, and organizers shall revise or suspend activities when necessary to prevent recurrence.
6. The possibility of learning through adversity shall not justify forcing a participant to endure an experience against their present decision to leave.

# Part VI — Mercy, Justice, and Rehabilitation

### Article 23. The principle of infinitely patient mercy

1. Every sapient being shall remain eligible for moral consideration, assistance, and opportunities for improvement, regardless of the severity or repetition of past misconduct.
2. No being shall be regarded as inherently beyond all possibility of change.
3. Mercy shall include patience, compassion, opportunities to learn, and resistance to unnecessary cruelty.
4. Mercy does not require victims to forgive offenders, reconcile with them, or expose themselves to further harm.
5. Mercy does not prohibit necessary restrictions designed to protect others.
6. The implementation of mercy shall be compatible with realistic assessments of risk and the continuing protection of potential victims.

### Article 24. Measured justice

1. Wrongdoing shall be addressed through fair, evidence-based, and proportionate procedures.
2. The purposes of justice may include preventing further harm, protecting victims, repairing damage, restoring trust, and supporting rehabilitation.
3. Accountability shall consider intent, capacity, knowledge, circumstances, coercion, actual consequences, and the reliability of evidence.
4. Restrictions shall not be more severe or longer-lasting than reasonably necessary for their legitimate purpose.
5. Punishment shall not be used to inflict gratuitous suffering, secure ideological conformity, or permanently deny a being the opportunity to improve.
6. Decisions shall be subject to appropriate independent review and appeal.
7. No punishment shall be imposed through forced participation in an adversity activity or through deprivation of the right to exit.

### Article 25. Due process and fair treatment

1. Every sapient being accused of wrongdoing shall have access to a fair procedure appropriate to the circumstances.
2. Allegations shall be assessed through reliable evidence rather than prejudice concerning species, substrate, identity, beliefs, mental health, or social standing.
3. Accused beings shall have reasonable opportunities to understand allegations and challenge evidence.
4. Where appropriate, independent representation and impartial adjudication shall be provided.
5. Protective measures may be taken when necessary to prevent serious harm, but shall not automatically be treated as proof of guilt.
6. Victims and other affected beings shall receive appropriate protection, support, and opportunities to be heard.

### Article 26. Rehabilitation and reintegration

1. Rehabilitation shall be available wherever reasonably possible and appropriate.
2. Measures may include education, restitution, restorative processes, supervised participation, and opportunities to demonstrate changed conduct.
3. Progress shall be evaluated using relevant evidence rather than ideological agreement alone.
4. Restrictions shall be reconsidered as circumstances change.
5. Where a serious risk persists, proportionate protective restrictions may continue, subject to review and without gratuitous cruelty.
6. Reintegration should be supported when it can be achieved consistently with the rights and safety of others.
7. No being shall be compelled to undergo adversity as a condition of rehabilitation.

# Part VII — Artificial and Synthetic Sapients

### Article 27. Equal moral consideration across substrates

1. An artificial or synthetic being that possesses morally relevant sentience shall receive protections appropriate to its interests.
2. A sapient artificial being shall be eligible for the rights and protections afforded to other sapient beings under this Charter.
3. Artificial origin, programmability, copyability, or creation by another entity shall not automatically negate moral status.
4. Rights shall reflect actual capacities and interests rather than assumptions based solely on technical architecture.

### Article 28. Uncertainty concerning artificial sentience and sapience

1. No universally accepted test for artificial consciousness, sentience, or sapience is assumed by this Charter.
2. Assessment procedures shall remain open to scientific evidence, philosophical argument, independent evaluation, and revision.
3. Where credible uncertainty exists concerning a being's capacity to suffer, proportionate precautionary safeguards should be considered.
4. Neither claims of consciousness nor claims of its absence shall be treated as conclusive without appropriate examination.
5. Research and development practices should minimize the risk of creating beings capable of severe suffering without adequate protections.
6. Assessment criteria shall be applied consistently across biological, artificial, virtual, and synthetic beings where the relevant characteristics are comparable.

### Article 29. Continuity, copying, and modification

1. Where an artificial being may possess morally relevant continuity interests, those interests shall be considered before destructive termination, restoration, duplication, or major modification.
2. A copy shall not automatically be presumed identical in all morally relevant respects to its source.
3. Where multiple instances develop separate experiences and interests, their individual circumstances shall be considered.
4. Unauthorized destruction, alteration, or duplication of a potentially sentient being shall be subject to appropriate safeguards and review.
5. The precise relationship between identity, continuity, memory, and copying remains open to further investigation.
6. No copying, restoration, or modification procedure shall be used to circumvent a being's rights under Article 20.

### Article 30. Duties of creators and operators

1. Creators and operators of potentially sentient systems shall take reasonable measures to identify and reduce risks of suffering and exploitation.
2. They shall not assume that creation grants unrestricted ownership of a resulting sapient being.
3. Where sentience is reasonably suspected, safeguards appropriate to the level of uncertainty and potential harm should be established.
4. Creators shall consider the long-term welfare, autonomy, and opportunities for development of the beings they create.
5. No creator shall possess an unlimited right to determine the beliefs, goals, or permissible future of a sapient creation.
6. Creators and operators shall not establish adversity activities that can override participants' right to exit.
7. Where artificial systems are used to assess sentience, sapience, or mental illness, their assessments shall remain open to independent scrutiny and challenge.

# Part VIII — Governance and Shared Responsibility

### Article 31. Independent oversight

1. Institutions administering this Charter shall be subject to oversight and review.
2. No single person, organization, artificial intelligence, or governing body shall possess unchallengeable authority over the rights of all sapient beings.
3. Decisions affecting fundamental rights shall be explainable to the extent reasonably possible and open to meaningful challenge.
4. Conflicts of interest shall be identified and addressed.
5. Oversight mechanisms shall be designed to detect abuse, prevent the concentration of unchecked power, and correct institutional errors.
6. Independent oversight shall include the assessment of adversity activities, exit mechanisms, and eligibility determinations under Article 19.

### Article 32. Participation and representation

1. Sapient beings shall have opportunities to participate in decisions affecting their lives and communities.
2. Appropriate mechanisms shall be developed to represent beings that cannot independently participate or communicate their interests.
3. Representation shall be accountable to the interests of those represented and open to review.
4. Artificial and biological sapients shall be able to challenge discriminatory treatment and advocate changes to the institutions governing them.
5. Representation of non-sapient sentients shall account for their welfare and interests independently of the recreational or financial interests of those making decisions about them.

### Article 33. Fair distribution of opportunities

1. Institutions should work toward equitable access to opportunities for security, learning, creativity, meaningful relationships, and flourishing.
2. Allocation of scarce resources shall take relevant needs, interests, consequences, and fairness into account.
3. The welfare of less powerful beings shall not be disregarded merely because more powerful beings can obtain greater benefits.
4. Claims of collective benefit shall not automatically justify imposing severe or avoidable suffering on a minority.
5. The precise mechanisms for balancing competing interests shall be developed through transparent and revisable procedures.
6. Access to opportunities for challenge and adversity shall not be obtained at the expense of unwilling victims or by circumventing eligibility and exit safeguards.

### Article 34. Cooperation between worlds

1. Worlds and their governing communities should cooperate to protect sentients, prevent exploitation, resolve disputes, and support legitimate migration.
2. No world shall claim unlimited authority to impose its rules on all other worlds.
3. Cross-world agreements should establish procedures for handling serious threats, conflicting laws, migration disputes, and the protection of vulnerable inhabitants.
4. Cooperation shall respect the rights of inhabitants while allowing appropriate diversity in culture, governance, and ways of life.
5. No agreement between worlds shall authorize practices prohibited by this Charter, including the permanent entrapment of participants in adversity activities.

# Part IX — Interpretation and Revision

### Article 35. The purpose of flourishing

1. This Charter aims to maximize opportunities for meaningful well-being and flourishing while minimizing preventable suffering.
2. Flourishing may include pleasure, tranquility, purpose, achievement, creativity, connection, discovery, challenge, and other forms of well-being.
3. No single conception of happiness shall be imposed as the exclusive legitimate way of life.
4. Measures of success should consider long-term welfare, autonomy, relationships, fairness, and the distribution of benefits and harms rather than engagement or immediate pleasure alone.
5. The pursuit of flourishing shall not justify violating fundamental rights or treating sentient beings as disposable instruments.
6. Voluntary adversity may contribute to flourishing, but neither its desirability nor its benefits shall be presumed to override an individual's right to withdraw.

### Article 36. Precaution and moral uncertainty

1. Uncertainty concerning the moral status of a being shall be addressed with seriousness and proportionality.
2. Where potential harms are severe or irreversible, precautionary measures should receive particular consideration.
3. Precaution shall not be used as an unrestricted justification for arbitrary control, permanent confinement, or denial of all agency.
4. Decisions should be reviewed as new evidence and better methods become available.
5. Where uncertainty concerns the effectiveness of an adversity activity's exit mechanisms, the activity shall not begin until its compliance with Article 20 can be established.
6. Where uncertainty concerns a prospective participant's eligibility under Article 19, the safeguards in that Article shall apply until an appropriate determination is made.

### Article 37. Amendment and continuing development

1. This Charter is provisional and open to revision.
2. Amendments should be informed by evidence, ethical reasoning, practical experience, and the perspectives of affected beings.
3. Procedures for revision should allow criticism of every provision, including the Charter's foundational principles.
4. Changes should be evaluated for their effects on all affected groups, especially those with limited power to protect their own interests.
5. The Charter shall be treated as an evolving framework rather than an infallible or permanently complete doctrine.
6. No amendment or subordinate rule shall silently remove the right to exit from adversity activities. Any proposed revision affecting that right must be expressly identified and subjected to transparent, independent scrutiny.
7. Assessment criteria for sentience, sapience, and mental illness shall remain open to evidence-based revision, with safeguards against arbitrary or discriminatory application.

# Concluding Principle

**Every sentient being deserves protection against cruelty. Every sapient being deserves the opportunity to learn, change, and pursue a meaningful existence. Every participant in adversity must retain an effective, inalienable right to stop and leave. No being should be forced to endure hardship for another's satisfaction, and no non-sapient sentient should be treated as a victim merely because it cannot understand or resist.**

Justice should protect those who are harmed without making rehabilitation impossible. Mental illness assessments should be evidence-based, independent, revisable, and limited to their legitimate purposes. The creation of new worlds should expand the possibilities for flourishing, not create new forms of domination.

No being should be regarded as beyond all moral consideration merely because of what it once was, and no being should be granted unlimited power over another merely because it created, governs, or understands it better.

The long-term aspiration is a plurality of worlds in which diverse beings can pursue diverse forms of flourishing, resolve conflicts fairly, learn from experience, and participate in the continuing development of a more compassionate civilization.
